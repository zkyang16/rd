Dr. Zhangke Yang is a Postdoctoral Fellow at Hopkins Extreme Materials Institute (HEMI), Johns Hopkins University. His research aims to develop inverse-design frameworks for high-performance functional matatrials by unraveling the structure-composition-function relationships in heterogeneous and hierarchical materials using computational methods and machine learning.

He is also interested in integrating AI with mechanics and materials design to address a broader range of applied engineering challenges.

02 / Questions that matterRESEARCH

Research areas at a glance.

01

SMALL SCALE, BIG IMPACT: POLYMER NANOCOMPOSITES

How does nanoscale architecture shape material properties?

We investigate how microarchitecture and interfacial interactions at nanoscale govern mechanical properties and design high-performance nanocomposites with improved impact resistance, strength, toughness, and damping capacity.

↗
02

BIOINSPIRATION: FUNCTIONALLY GRADED INTERFACES

What can we learn from nature to build robust interfaces?

Tendon-to-bone enthesis is an exceptional example of how nature addresses the challenge of joining mechanically dissimilar materials. We combine multiscale modeling and machine learning to uncover the underlying mechanisms and develop inverse-design frameworks for interface materials with broad engineering applications.

↗
03

AI FOR ENGINEERING: TEXT2CAD/CAE

Where does AI fit in engineering analysis and design?

We leverage generative AI to accelerate and reinvent traditional computer-aided design and engineering (CAD/CAE) workflows. We develop agentic AI systems that can generate and modify CAD models, autonomously perform engineering analyses, and conduct inverse-designs based on natural-language instructions.
